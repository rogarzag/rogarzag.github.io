RappiPlus — Business Performance Analysis

Business Performance, Customer Behavior and Checkout Experiment

Combined data-quality review, revenue and product profitability analysis, conversion-funnel and cohort-retention analysis, and checkout A/B test evaluation. The project uses Python, SQL, statistical testing and Tableau to translate multiple data sources into business findings and recommendations.

Project repository ↗Tableau dashboard ↗Analysis notebook ↗

Business questions

  • Can the available order and event data be trusted?
  • What do revenue and available product costs show about product-level profit?
  • Which products and categories contribute to performance?
  • Where do users drop off in the conversion funnel, and how does retention evolve?
  • Did the checkout interface experiment improve conversion?

Adjusted operating view

The raw dataset reported $51.99M in revenue. A small number of extreme-quantity records had a disproportionate effect, accounting for 81.45% of reported revenue and 91.12% of known product cost. The dashboard therefore presents an adjusted view that excludes extreme and invalid quantities to better represent typical operating performance.

Data quality and preparation

  • Loaded and prepared order-level data, converted date fields and checked missing values and duplicates.
  • Flagged invalid and unusually large quantities and compared raw with adjusted sales results.
  • Joined product costs to assess product-level profitability.
  • Retained 300 orders (1.20%) with unknown country because the records contained valid sales information and there was insufficient evidence to assign another location.
  • Kept marketing spend separate from product-level profit because the data sources had different levels of granularity.

Dashboard deliverables

Business recommendations

  • Review the unusual high-quantity orders before using raw revenue totals for planning; keep raw and adjusted views distinct.
  • Investigate the checkout-to-payment-information step after validating event tracking.
  • Keep the existing checkout version as the reference; gather more evidence before recommending the redesign on conversion grounds.
  • Review sales and product profit together rather than prioritizing products by unit sales alone.
  • Use cohort differences as a basis for further retention investigation, not as evidence of causal drivers.

Tools and analytical methods

Python: pandas, data cleaning, validation and exploratory analysis. SQL: funnel analysis, cohort retention and conditional aggregation. Statistics: two-proportion z-test and hypothesis testing. Tableau: KPI dashboards, filters, interactive views and business storytelling.
